Experiment E model on the untouched FracAtlas external benchmark (N=4,068).
The model achieves 99.64% accuracy on the internal BFRMX test set (N=560) but drops
to 49.90% accuracy on FracAtlas. This audit decomposes the exact source of this
degradation across anatomical regions, image properties, hardware implants, and visual
attention patterns using Grad-CAM and comparative tracking against Experiment F.

2. DOMINANT FAILURE MODES IDENTIFIED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. ASYMMETRIC FALSE POSITIVE DOMINANCE (The Primary Pathology):
   - Normal X-rays in FracAtlas are falsely predicted as fractures in 1,862 out of 3,355 cases (55.50% FPR).
   - In contrast, actual fractures are detected with high sensitivity (75.32% Recall, only 176 False Negatives).
   - The root cause is an over-sensitive fracture activation trigger when exposed to novel acquisition noise.

3. GRAD-CAM ATTENTION ANALYSIS (EXPLAINABILITY AUDIT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Grad-CAM visualizations at the pre-pooling layer (norm5) reveal distinct visual behaviors:

1. True Positives (Correct Fractures):
   - Sharp, concentrated focal heatmaps tightly locked onto cortical line disruptions, displaced fragments, and acute fracture lines.
   - DG-CBAM successfully combines spatial and channel attention to highlight authentic trauma.

2. High-Confidence False Positives (Normal Scans Mistaken for Fractures):
   - Saliency focuses on: (a) overlapping joint spaces (e.g. wrist carpal overlaps, knee joint margins),
     (b) radiographic collimation borders and marker edges, and (c) high-contrast cortical trabecular textures.
   - In normal anatomy with high bone density or exposure variations, natural overlap edges trigger fracture detectors.

3. High-Confidence False Negatives (Missed Fractures):
   - Occur predominantly on: (a) non-displaced hairline fractures without gap displacement, and (b) partial anatomy at image margins.
   - Heatmap diffuses across general bone shaft rather than localizing the hairline lesion.

4. COMPARATIVE LESSON FROM EXPERIMENT F
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When trained with heavy random domain-robust augmentation (Exp F):
  - Recall increased from 75.32% to 89.34% (fixed 100 False Negatives).
  - HOWEVER, False Positives exploded from 1,862 to 2,489 (+627 new FPs, a +33.67% surge).
  - Net accuracy dropped from 49.90% to 36.95%.
  - SCIENTIFIC TAKEAWAY: Naive pixel/contrast jittering caused the model to become even more hyper-sensitive,
    lowering its internal feature threshold for fracture triggers. Heavy augmentation alone cannot resolve domain shift.

5. EVIDENCE BREAKDOWN & SCIENTIFIC ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. What are the dominant failure patterns?
   - Overwhelmingly False Positives (55.5% of normal scans misclassified as fractured).

B. Is there evidence of domain shift?
   - YES (Definitive): In-domain BFRMX test accuracy is 99.64% with 0 False Positives, while out-of-domain FracAtlas
     yields 1,862 False Positives under identical preprocessing and identical decision threshold.

C. Is there evidence of anatomy/body-part mismatch?
   - YES (Moderate): Hand and multi-bone overlapping regions suffer higher error rates than long bones (Leg).

D. Is there evidence of image-quality/acquisition mismatch?
   - YES (Definitive): Differences in X-ray machine exposure, collimation borders, and soft-tissue contrast profiles
     between hospital source datasets drive spurious activations.

E. Does Grad-CAM show anatomically meaningful attention or background/artifact attention in the errors?
   - BOTH: False positives show attention on real anatomical joint interfaces (overlapping bones) as well as
     exposure edge artifacts at collimator boundaries.

F. What type of next experiment would be scientifically justified?
   - An anatomical or feature-alignment approach (e.g. Domain-Adversarial Neural Network / DANN, or Multi-Scale Joint
     Contrastive Learning) rather than superficial random color/contrast jittering.
