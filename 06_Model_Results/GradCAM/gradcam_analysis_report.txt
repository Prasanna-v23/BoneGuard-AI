1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & RESEARCH OBJECTIVE
================================================================================
The objective of this analysis is to evaluate the spatial explainability and 
visual attention properties of the three trained deep learning architectures:
  1. DenseNet121 Baseline
  2. DenseNet121 + Standard CBAM
  3. DenseNet121 + Proposed DG-CBAM (Disagreement-Guided CBAM)

Using standard Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM; Selvaraju 
et al., ICCV 2017), we investigate whether each model focuses on plausibly 
salient anatomical fracture lines versus background artifacts, soft tissue, 
orthopedic hardware, or radiographic text markers.

IMPORTANT RESEARCH ATTRIBUTION:
  - The proposed novelty of this research is strictly DG-CBAM (Disagreement-Guided
    Convolutional Block Attention Module).
  - Grad-CAM is a standard, supporting qualitative interpretability tool.
  - Grad-CAM provides qualitative visual evidence and does NOT constitute clinical
    proof of fracture diagnosis.

3. GRAD-CAM METHOD & TARGET LAYER IDENTIFICATION
================================================================================
Standard Grad-CAM Formulation:
  1. Input radiograph X in R^(3 x 224 x 224) is passed through the network.
  2. Unnormalized class score y^c for target class c (c=0: FRACTURE) is extracted.
  3. Gradients of y^c with respect to feature activation map A^k of the target
     convolutional layer are computed: dy^c / dA^k.
  4. Importance weights alpha_k^c are calculated via global average pooling:
         alpha_k^c = (1 / Z) * sum_i sum_j (dy^c / dA_ij^k)
  5. Saliency map L_Grad-CAM^c is obtained as the rectified linear combination:
         L_Grad-CAM^c = ReLU( sum_k alpha_k^c * A^k )
  6. Heatmap is normalized to [0, 1], bilinearly/cubically interpolated to 224x224,
     and overlaid onto the unnormalized radiograph using the JET colormap (alpha=0.45).

Target Layer Rationale:
  - Target Layer for all 3 models: `model.features.norm5`
  - Architecture location: Final batch-normalization layer immediately following
    DenseBlock 4 and preceding the final ReLU activation and Global Average Pooling.
  - Spatial resolution: 7 x 7 feature grid with 1,024 semantic feature channels.
  - Why this layer: It represents the richest, highest-level spatial representation
    before global spatial collapse. In CBAM and DG-CBAM, the Stage 4 attention
    refinement is applied inside denseblock4/transition, culminating in norm5.

5. QUALITATIVE & QUANTITATIVE OBSERVATIONS
================================================================================

5.1. BFRMX INTERNAL DATASET OBSERVATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- True Positives (Fracture Cases):
  * All three models achieve near-saturated confidence (>99.8% fracture probability).
  * DenseNet121 baseline produces broad, diffuse activation fields covering both the
    fracture site and adjacent cortical bone.
  * Standard CBAM sharpens the activation field but occasionally places secondary
    hotspots on non-fractured bone contours.
  * DG-CBAM produces the most compact, anatomically localized attention peak,
    centering high-intensity heatmaps directly over cortical discontinuities.

- True Negatives (Normal Bone Cases):
  * All three models correctly assign <0.1% fracture probability.
  * Grad-CAM heatmaps for the fracture class (c=0) show negligible or uniform low-energy
    background activation, confirming absence of false positive triggers on clean internal data.

5.2. FRACATLAS EXTERNAL CROSS-DOMAIN OBSERVATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FracAtlas presents substantial domain shifts (different X-ray scanners, resolutions,
patient demographics, contrast levels, and pervasive radiographic text markers).

Current Measured External Performance:
  - DenseNet121:     Acc = 41.30%, Recall = 76.86%, Spec = 33.74%, ROC-AUC = 0.6104
  - DenseNet121+CBAM: Acc = 32.52%, Recall = 86.12%, Spec = 21.13%, ROC-AUC = 0.5861
  - DenseNet121+DGCBAM: Acc = 40.09%, Recall = 82.05%, Spec = 31.18%, ROC-AUC = 0.6336

Grad-CAM Insights into External Generalization:
- True Positives (Cases 09-12):
  * In cross-domain fracture cases (e.g., IMG0000019, IMG0000025, IMG0000044), DG-CBAM
    maintains high fracture confidence (98.1% - 99.9%) while preserving focused attention
    on the actual traumatic lesion.
  * Baseline DenseNet121 exhibits wider spatial dispersion, often spreading heatmaps
    across entire joint spaces.

- False Positives (Cases 17-21):
  * All models suffer from elevated false-positive rates on FracAtlas due to severe domain
    shift and high prevalence of degenerative changes, overlapping bone margins, and projection angles.
  * Baseline DenseNet121 and CBAM frequently trigger false alarms (e.g., IMG0000000, IMG0000001)
    by focusing heavily on anatomical joint spaces (e.g., carpals, wrist joint lines) and dense
    cortical overlaps that mimic fracture radiolucencies.
  * In Case 20 (IMG0000003), Standard CBAM correctly suppressed the false alarm (predicting TN with
    46.1% fracture prob), whereas Baseline and DG-CBAM predicted FP (62.8% and 91.1%).
  * Visual inspection reveals that FP heatmaps consistently localize to high-contrast bone-edge
    interfaces, demonstrating that false positives stem from radiographic texture misinterpretation
    rather than random background noise.

- False Negatives / Subtle Fractures (Cases 22-25):
  * In Case 22 (IMG0000151), all three models produced False Negatives (predicting NO_FRACTURE with
    fracture probabilities: Baseline 35.2%, CBAM 26.6%, DG-CBAM 7.4%).
  * In Cases 23-25 (IMG0000189, IMG0000319, IMG0000351), Baseline and CBAM predicted FRACTURE
    (over-calling sensitivity), while DG-CBAM predicted NO_FRACTURE (more conservative specificity).
  * Heatmap examination for missed fractures shows that the models fail to generate localized
    gradients over hairline/non-displaced fissures when cortical disruption is minimal.

5.3. ORTHOPEDIC HARDWARE & METALLIC IMPLANTS (Cases 26-28)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Analysis of radiographs with orthopedic hardware (plates, screws, intramedullary rods):
  * Standard CBAM frequently places secondary attention peaks directly on metallic screw
    heads and plate boundaries due to extreme pixel contrast.
  * DG-CBAM's disagreement refinement mechanism (|Mc - Ms|) detects spatial-channel conflict
    at metallic interfaces, suppressing artificial over-excitation on hardware and re-orienting
    focus toward the surrounding peri-implant bone margin and fracture line.
  * CAUTION: Hardware attention is not automatically an error; in post-operative imaging,
    the fracture line often directly underlies or borders the fixation plate.

5.4. TEXT ANNOTATIONS, ARROWS & RADIOGRAPHIC MARKERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- In images containing orientation markers ('L', 'R') and radiologist annotations:
  * Baseline DenseNet121 occasionally exhibits faint gradient leakage towards peripheral text.
  * In both CBAM and DG-CBAM, spatial attention Ms heavily suppresses image borders and non-anatomical
    regions, effectively eliminating marker shortcut learning.
  * No model exhibited catastrophic shortcut failure where classification was solely driven by text.

6. DG-CBAM MULTI-STAGE INTERNAL ATTENTION BREAKDOWN
================================================================================
For DG-CBAM, we extracted and visualized intermediate attention tensors across all 4 stages:
  - Channel Attention: Mc in R^(C x 1 x 1)
  - Spatial Attention: Ms in R^(1 x H x W)
  - Disagreement Map: D = |Mc - Ms| in R^(C x H x W)
  - Refined Attention: M_new = Ms + gamma * D in R^(C x H x W)

Key Findings:
  1. Stage 1 (256 ch, 56x56): Spatial attention Ms acts as a coarse anatomical mask, isolating
     the bone structure from black background air. Disagreement D highlights edge transitions.
     Learned gamma = 0.08985 provides gentle edge enhancement.
  2. Stage 2 (512 ch, 28x28): Channel attention Mc selects intermediate texture features.
     Disagreement D isolates structural inconsistencies. Learned gamma = 0.11714.
  3. Stage 3 (1024 ch, 14x14): Attention begins localizing specifically to candidate lesion sites.
     Disagreement D reaches peak magnitude where channel and spatial feature priorities diverge.
     Learned gamma = 0.11823.
  4. Stage 4 (1024 ch, 7x7): Final refined attention M_new tightly bounds the focal diagnostic region.
     Learned gamma = 0.11574 adds ~11.5% disagreement weighting, sharpening the final pre-pooling representation.

This multi-stage visual breakdown confirms the technical mechanics of the proposed DG-CBAM novelty
without modifying weights or training.

7. LIMITATIONS & CLINICAL SAFETY NOTICE
================================================================================
- Qualitative Nature: Grad-CAM generates coarse (7x7 upsampled) qualitative heatmaps.
  It does NOT constitute mathematical proof of causal feature attribution.
- Resolution Limit: Small hairline fractures (<2mm) fall below the receptive field granularity
  of 7x7 feature maps, leading to diffuse or ambiguous heatmaps.
- Clinical Use: BoneGuard AI is strictly an experimental Clinical Decision Support (CDS) tool.
  It is NOT an autonomous medical device and CANNOT replace expert radiologist review.
